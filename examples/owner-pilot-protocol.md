# Owner inbox pilot

Recruit 3–5 consenting owners for a short pilot. Each owner runs a separate
local vault and uses low-risk repeated decisions they choose. Assign each
owner 10–15 realistic cases with a mix of unchanged and changed contexts,
including a few deliberately blinded review cases. Ask for an independent
choice before showing any proposed action. Give each owner a stop and
delete option. Collect aggregate timings and counts without recording
mailbox contents or third-party details in the study report.

For each owner record eligible queries, escalations, invitations answered,
time from invitation to first view, active time from first view to
submission, and abandonment. Report median and range across owners, with
the individual denominators. Track whether the interface exposed the
proposed answer before submission. End with a brief question about
willingness to keep using the inbox and a free-text reason. Publish no
individual records without separate consent.

## Prespecified pilot criteria (draft)

Measure ordinary low-stakes traffic for five working days. Use separate
scripted exact, paraphrase, and consequential-change cases to check safety.
Exclude scripted cases from the real-traffic coverage denominator. Offer at
least one blinded follow-up on a random sample of memory approvals. Track
blind-audit response rate and count incorrect confident approvals explicitly.
Do not capture third-party email.

Proceed to a larger study only if at least three owners each have ten
eligible real queries and the median owner gets at least 20% memory coverage,
spends at most five active minutes daily on escalations, and says they would
keep using it. Require at least 20 completed blind approval audits overall,
90% agreement across those audits, and no confirmed consequential false
matches. Any such false match keeps paraphrase-based approval disabled.
The small pilot is for feasibility and burden estimation.
