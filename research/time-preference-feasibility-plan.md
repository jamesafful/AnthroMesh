# Time-preference development feasibility plan

Freeze before reading choice outcomes. Use the existing 100-person model-selection split recovered from the pinned early-wave participant IDs and original hash seeds. Read only the 60 audited early-wave response columns. Read no Wave 4 answers. This is a development-only, within-task analysis. Do not open the earlier holdouts or use risk, trust, or pricing as transfer targets.

Require 60 complete participants among 100. A complete participant has a valid binary response in each of six lists. Report complete-case counts by matched schedule and multiple switchers (more than one direction change within a list), then retain every valid row regardless of switch pattern. Report selection by schedule completion and compare observed switch rates in complete and incomplete cases.

Fit a weekly log discount rate to noisy binary choices using log payment ratios, a common fixed logistic noise scale of 0.12, and a prespecified nonnegative rate grid with exponential shrinkage prior. Fit the alternative with an additional nonnegative log immediacy penalty. Use posterior predictive probabilities. Hold out each whole matched list pair. Compare person-specific exponential and immediacy models with pooled exponential trained on other participants and nonheld schedules. Report participant-mean paired log loss and 95% participant bootstrap intervals with 5,000 draws.

Require all pairwise Spearman correlations of the separately fitted delayed-only schedule posterior means to reach 0.70. This is an exploratory minimum, not evidence of individual decision reliability. Rank consistency is not absolute agreement or a temporal retest. Require at least 70% of complete cases to have an all-delayed-fit central 90% interval no wider than 0.20 weekly log units and a posterior standard deviation no greater than 75% of the prior standard deviation. These two tests and sample completeness must pass before using the signal to motivate a transfer efficacy study. The registry remains research-only in every case.

Prefer the immediacy model only if it lowers held-pair mean log loss by at least 0.01 nats per row versus exponential, with a positive lower paired interval bound. Otherwise retain exponential only if feasibility passes. Repeat at noise scales 0.06 and 0.24 and with one common fixed order offset of −0.15 or +0.15 logits on now-list rows as sensitivity analyses. Opposite offsets model opposite possible order effects. They cannot identify actual order or incentives. No further tuning.
